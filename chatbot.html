EquiStaff · AI concierge · for build

Every state, and the rule it enforces

V1 is a scripted assistant, not a self-learning AI. Every path is written in advance, and anything it cannot answer goes to “book a call with Kaitlin.” It answers with live numbers, helps people finish a profile or a job post, collects employer inquiries and hands off — it never generates an answer that is not in the approved content.

Rule numbers below (R1–R12) are the hard rules from EquiStaff Chatbot: Engineering Handoff (V1), 19 October. That document is the specification; this page is what it looks like.

What she asked for — six capabilities

Answer with live numbers

The company, the board, the recruiting process, what the published services cost — plus live stats like open roles and jobseekers in the last 30 days.

Troubleshoot accounts

Password resets, subscribing, cancelling, search tips.

Improve what people write

Prompt a jobseeker to finish their profile and fill the fields from their answers. Spot a thin job advert and offer to rewrite it.

Capture leads

Push employers toward the recruiting service, especially those who never signed up or whose advert has run over 30 days. Collect the brief and save it.

Book time and hand off

Share the booking calendar — a different link for a jobseeker than for an employer.

Nudge and match, but not too well

Her words: it should work, “but not work SO well that it makes the recruiting service obsolete.”

Still unanswered — the live list is in the handoff document

  • Money. Published prices yes. Can it ever discuss a bespoke recruiting fee? Designed below as a hard no.
  • Live data. Stats, profile fields, resumes and lead storage are reads and writes against the platform. The chatbot is an integration, not a widget.
  • The throttle. How good can matching get before it cannibalises the recruiting service? We need a rule, not a feeling.
  • Identity. Does it say it is AI? Designed below as yes.
  • Spanish. A meaningful share of barn staff are Spanish-speaking. Far cheaper to plan for now.
  • Resume accountability. If it writes someone a resume, does a person review it before it attaches to an application?

The states

State 01

Opening

Asks the same single question the landing page asks, so the two doors stay consistent wherever someone arrives.

Evie at EquiStaffAn AI assistant · here around the clock
A person is one tap away, any time

Evening. I can help with most things here, and put you through to Kaitlin’s team whenever you’d rather talk to a person.

To point you the right way — are you hiring, or looking for work?

R8 · R11: it names itself as AI in the header, and the route to a human is visible before the first question — not buried after a failure.

State 02

Answering with live numbers

Her first requirement. The figures are read from the platform at answer time, never written into the response text.

Evie at EquiStaffAn AI assistant · here around the clock
How many barn manager jobs are open right now?

14 barn manager roles are open today, and 6 of them include housing.

Across the whole board there are 214 open roles, and 203 jobseekers signed in over the last 30 days.

Read live from the job board. If the number is stale or unavailable the assistant says so rather than guessing.

R5: a number it cannot read is a number it will not invent. No figure is ever hard-coded into a reply.

State 03

The money boundary

The wish list asks it to answer on costs. At kickoff the line was that it must never commit to a number. Both hold if published prices and bespoke fees are separated.

Evie at EquiStaffAn AI assistant · here around the clock
What would it cost me to have you fill a head trainer role?

I can give you the published prices: posting a job yourself is a monthly subscription, and having us write and run the advert is priced per role.

A full recruiting search is quoted per role, and I won’t put a number on it — it depends on the seniority and how hard the brief is. Kaitlin quotes those herself, before any work starts.

Want me to set that up? It takes about fifteen minutes.

R3: published prices yes, the direct-hire percentage never. A refusal always arrives with the next step attached, so it reads as routing rather than stonewalling.

R9: every employer request goes to Kaitlin, never a candidate list. The inquiry is written to the lead register with its source and a timestamp, and it lands even if the person leaves before the last answer.

State 05

Handing off to a person

A different calendar depending on who is asking — her requirement. The assistant passes the conversation across rather than making them start again.

R2 · R11: the transcript travels with the handoff. Asking a person to repeat themselves to a human after talking to a bot is the thing that makes people hate bots.

Phase 2 — designed, not built

The bot never speaks first in V1. This is here so the event hooks get built now rather than retrofitted.

Phase 2 · not in V1

The thirty-day nudge

Her named trigger: an advert that has run over 30 days, or an employer who never subscribed. The assistant opens the conversation rather than waiting to be asked.

Evie at EquiStaffAn AI assistant · here around the clock

Your part-time groom advert has been live 41 days and has 2 applicants.

That usually means the advert, not the market. I can rewrite it for you now — or Kaitlin can take it over as a managed search and bring you four people.

“Leave it for now” is a real option and is not asked again for 14 days.

Not in V1. The handoff document puts the bot speaking first — any proactive trigger — in Phase 2. Designed now because the event hooks should be built in V1 so this drops in later without rework. The opt-out is a first-class option: declined once, not asked again for 14 days.
